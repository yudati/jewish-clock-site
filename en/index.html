What's inside

Built for people who live by the Jewish calendar — at home, in shul and on the road.

🕰️

Halachic times

Dawn, sunrise, latest Shema, chatzot, mincha, sunset and nightfall — for any place in Israel or worldwide, by the method of Rav Ovadia, Rav Tzikoni or Rav Melamed. Including the latest (bedi'avad) time for each prayer.

📖

Complete siddur

Shacharit, Mincha, Maariv, blessings, Rosh Chodesh, Chol HaMoed, Selichot and the Omer — in Ashkenaz, Sefard and Edot HaMizrach, showing only today's additions. Handwriting and STaM fonts.

⏰

Smart alarm clock

Wake up by halachic time ("20 minutes before sunrise"), not just by the clock. Shabbat and Yom Tov mode: the alarm rings and stops by itself — no snooze, no touching.

🕍

Find a minyan

Synagogues and prayer times near you, from GoDaven and the national prayer-times project of Nedarim Plus — with distance, navigation and phone. Plus your own personal minyan list.

📚

Daily learning & books

Daf Yomi, Chok LeYisrael, Shnayim Mikra (each verse twice plus Onkelos), Pirkei Avot, Tehillim — and free search across the whole Jewish library via Sefaria.

📅

Hebrew calendar

Holidays, fasts, Torah portions and your personal dates (Hebrew birthdays, yahrzeits, anniversaries) with reminders, and sync to your phone's calendar.

🕯️

Shabbat preparation

Candle-lighting and havdalah times, an Erev Shabbat reminder and checklist, a Shabbat widget, and automatic Shabbat mode for all alarms.

🔒

Private. No ads.

No ads and no tracking. Your settings and alarms stay on your device. Full details in the privacy policy.

Frequently asked questions

Is the app free?

Yes, completely. Every feature is open to everyone, with no ads and no subscription. If you'd like to support development, you can make a one-time donation (₪18 to ₪100) through Google Play from Settings › Donate. A donation doesn't unlock anything — it's simply a thank-you.

What language is the app in?

The app's interface is in Hebrew, and the siddur and texts are in Hebrew and Aramaic. It works for any location in the world, including the diaspora (second day of Yom Tov, diaspora Torah readings).

Are the times accurate?

Times are calculated astronomically for the location and method you chose, on the stringent side. They are a helpful tool, not a halachic ruling — when in doubt, compare with a printed calendar and ask a rabbi.

What happens on Shabbat?

In Shabbat mode, an alarm set for Shabbat or Yom Tov rings and stops by itself after a set time, with no snooze, so you never need to touch the phone. Regular alarms are silenced automatically from candle lighting until Shabbat ends.

Where does the minyan data come from?

From GoDaven and the national prayer-times project of Nedarim Plus, and from OpenStreetMap. The data is provided by the communities themselves and may change — it's worth checking before you go.

Is there an iPhone version?

For now the app is available for Android only.
