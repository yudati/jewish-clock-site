1. The service

The App provides tools for the Jewish day: halachic time calculations, a siddur, an alarm clock, a Hebrew calendar, a minyan finder, daily learning and search of the sources. The App is a helpful tool only and does not replace a printed calendar, a rabbi or a halachic authority.

2. Times and halachic content — important

  • Halachic times are calculated astronomically for the location and method you chose, and shown on the stringent side. They may be off by a few minutes because of elevation, horizon, daylight-saving settings, location accuracy or differences between calculation methods. Where minutes matter (latest Shema, the start of Shabbat, the end of a fast and so on), compare with an authoritative printed calendar.
  • Prayer texts are based on open sources (mainly Sefaria and Wikisource) and were edited carefully, but errors may remain. Corrections are welcome.
  • Halachot, the "minute of Torah" and "Ask the sources" results are quotations and references to sources — not a halachic ruling. For any halachic question, ask a rabbi.
  • Israel / diaspora (second day of Yom Tov, Torah readings) is set by the chosen city. It's your responsibility to make sure the chosen city is correct.

3. Alarm clock and reminders

The App does its best to fire alarms and reminders on time, but they depend on the operating system, battery and do-not-disturb settings, system updates, volume and the state of the device. Don't rely on the App as your only means for events where being late or missing them could cause harm. We are not responsible for damage from an alarm that didn't ring, rang late or rang at the wrong time.

"Shabbat mode" silences or stops alarms according to the times and settings you chose. It's your responsibility to check in advance that the settings suit your custom and location.
